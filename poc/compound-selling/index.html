The philosophy

Something in your revenue org compounds. Right now it's the wrong thing.

Selling runs on context across several layers: who your customers are, how your team sells, and what your best people have learned doing it. Every call, deal, and quarter adds to all three.

The problem was never data entry. It's that this context is scattered — across a dozen systems, months of conversations, playbooks no one updates, and judgment that lives in your best people or nowhere at all. It's never assembled and never persists, so none of it compounds. Each quarter the team rebuilds what it already knew. Point AI at that and it reassembles the story from fragments — and its small errors compound into confident, wrong answers.

Compound selling flips what compounds. When every layer accumulates somewhere AI can actually use it, a won deal becomes a pattern, a lost one a warning, and a top rep's instinct becomes something the whole team can run. The compounding asset isn't the deal — it's the context. All of it.

What compounds today

Without a context layer, the liabilities compound — not the assets.

1

Errors compound.

Point AI at individual connectors and it gets fragments. It still has to infer which records, calls, and signals belong to the same customer — every query, from scratch.

The math95% right × 5 steps = 77%
Five mostly-right steps leave nearly one answer in four built on a flawed chain.
2

Costs compound.

Every prompt spends tokens rebuilding context that should already exist — account history, timelines, call evidence, Slack threads. The next prompt pays the tax again.

Token budget~90% context assembly
When most of a query is just reassembling the picture, you're paying to remember.
3

Brittleness compounds.

Every MCP server, API path, and schema change becomes infrastructure someone has to babysit. When it breaks, the answer breaks with it.

A real situation"Three days of 12-hour days."
"Our Salesforce MCP stopped working… and it pulled our VP RevOps off other work."

All three get worse the more you scale AI across the team. The one thing that should compound — what your team learns about each customer — is the one thing that doesn't.

The main loop

One loop, run on every account.

A human decides what matters. The system gathers, drafts, and remembers. Each pass leaves the next one easier.

Research → prep → engage → review → coach → compound
1

Research

The full account picture in one question — CRM, calls, email, calendar, Slack — identity resolved and ordered on a single timeline.

2

Prep

A brief that pulls from the last three calls, the latest email thread, the CRM notes, and the channel where your AE flagged a concern yesterday. Not one source. All of them.

3

Engage

The rep runs the meeting with complete context in hand — every open item, every relationship, every signal from the last touch.

4

Review

Pipeline and deal health graded against what's actually happening across every channel — not just what got typed into the CRM. The number, and the story behind it.

5

Coach

Coaching moments with timestamps from real calls, tied to outcomes. The best playbook spreads instead of staying in one head.

6

Compound

The step most teams skip. What the system surfaces this week gets logged. Next week it references it. Trends emerge. Each cycle sharpens the next.

What changes

Five beliefs to retire.

The problem is data entry.

It never was. It's fragmentation — context spread across systems, conversations, and months that nothing ever assembles. No amount of typing fixes that.

Customer data is the context.

It's one layer. How your team sells and what it has learned matter just as much — and live in even fewer places than the customer data does.

Connect more tools and AI gets smarter.

Five connectors give five fragments. Access isn't intelligence. The model still guesses how they fit — and pays for it every query.

Every answer is built from scratch.

Resolve the layers once. Then every brief, review, and analysis starts from the full picture instead of rebuilding it.

AI for sales means a chatbot.

Fast answers from fragments erode trust. The goal is AI reliable enough to deploy to the team — that cites its sources and flags what's missing.

One honest caveat

Not everything in revenue compounds.

A deal can be run perfectly and lost to a budget freeze, or fumbled and won because a competitor stumbled — so no single outcome proves a pattern. What compounds is the context: the account knowledge, the objection patterns, the picture of how your team actually sells. Build the layer that captures that, and the judgment gets better even when any one deal doesn't.
